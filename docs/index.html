The question

Is this line priced right?

Pick'em apps post a number for a player stat and you choose more or less. To break even on a typical multi-pick entry you need to be right roughly 55% of the time on every leg. A coin flip loses money.

So the real question is not "who wins?" It's: what is the actual chance this stat goes over the number, and is that chance high enough to matter? Apex Intelligence answers that hundreds of times a day, for tennis first and then ten other leagues.

    How to read a tennis board

    #1, #2… The spot. Boards are ranked by Win Probability plus 5 points for each outside signal that agrees.
    Total games MORE 17.5 The prop: will the match have more than 17.5 games? "Goblin" is the app's easier, lower line, which pays less.
    Win 80.7% The model's chance this pick hits. Top spots need 75%+.
    Score 87 The ranking number: Win Probability plus signal points.
    Honest 74% What picks like this have really hit, from graded history. It keeps the model's number in check.
    Book 20.5 · room +3 The sportsbook's total for the match and how far above the line it sits. More room is safer.
    Signals Outside checks that agree: other app's price, sportsbook room, low blowout risk, favorite strength, rest.
    🎯 play Passed the extra check Apex Intelligence runs on lines near 17.5 games.
    Built to be honest

    The model has to earn its numbers

    The hardest part wasn't making predictions. It was making sure the system can't fool me.

    Illustration of the idea, not real results. A point on the dashed line means "when it said 70%, it hit 70%."

    🧾

    Every pick gets graded

    Final scores settle every pick automatically, including voids for retirements and walkovers. Nothing is counted until it's graded.

    📏

    Honest probability

    Each category's real hit rate is shown next to the model's number, and categories that underperform get held off the boards automatically.

    🧪

    Backtest before shipping

    Rule changes are tested against saved history first. Research lives in its own repo with ~170 study scripts.

    🔔

    Change-only alerts

    Discord gets an alert when something actually changes: a line moves, a pick is pulled, a feed goes down. No walls of text.

    🛟

    Restore points

    Every rule change has a named go-back point and a one-switch undo, so experiments never put the live system at risk.

    🐕

    Watchdogs

    Heartbeats, a scheduler watchdog and data-health checks catch stale feeds or a stuck job before a bad board goes out.

    What I learned

    Lessons from building it

    The market is hard to beat

    A study across saved sportsbook prices found no lasting edge on match winners or total games. That changed the goal: use the books as a reality check, and look for edges in the smaller props instead.

    A great backtest can still lie

    Live picks ran hotter than graded results. Showing an "honest" number from real outcomes next to the model's number fixed more decisions than any new feature did.

    Data quality is the whole game

    Name collisions, players whose lines moved to a new date, wrong surfaces, a match graded from the wrong opponent. Most of the real work was making the data trustworthy.

    Make changes small and reversible

    Every change gets a switch to turn it off and a restore point. It made it safe to try ideas fast without breaking what already worked.
